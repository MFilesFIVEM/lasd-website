Official Training Bureau & Classroom

LASD Academy — Recruit Follow-Along Guide

A clean, simple, and structured classroom and training follow-along guide tailored for LASD Academy students. Review expectations, legal standards, radio protocols, and practical scenarios.

01

Module 1: Introduction & Expectations

The Academy Mindset

You are here to learn how to think, communicate, and act like a professional deputy. Decision-making and officer safety always come before speed.

Instructor Authority

Instructors have full authority during academy sessions.

Rule: Orders are followed first, and questions are discussed afterward.
Professional Conduct

Maintain composure under stress, respect staff and fellow recruits, and hold yourself accountable at all times during patrol and classroom settings.

02

Module 2: Legal Standards (Arrest, Search & Seizure)

Reasonable Suspicion "Check This Out" Level
  • What it is: More than a gut feeling; based on specific, articulable facts.
  • What you can do: Brief detention (investigative stop) and a pat-down/frisk for weapons only if you believe the subject is armed and dangerous.
  • What you cannot do: Full searches or arrests.
Probable Cause "Crime Committed" Level
  • What it is: Objective evidence establishing a reasonable belief that a crime was committed and the suspect committed it.
  • What you can do: Make a custodial arrest, conduct full searches incident to arrest, and apply for search/arrest warrants.
03

Module 3: Radio Communications & 10-Codes

Core Radio Rules
  • Keep transmissions short, calm, and clear.
  • Always identify your unit at the beginning and end when practical.
  • Listen before transmitting; emergency traffic (10-33) always takes priority.
Essential 10-Codes Reference
10-4 – Acknowledged
10-6 – Busy
10-7 – Out of Service
10-8 – In Service
10-20 – Location
10-28 – Registration Check
10-33 – Emergency Clearance
10-38 – Traffic Stop Check
10-97 – Arrived at Scene
10-98 – Finished Assignment
04

Module 4: Patrol Operations & Tactical Stops

Traffic Stops (Unknown Risk Procedure)
  1. Reason: Establish clear legal justification for the stop.
  2. Positioning: Offset or proper vehicle placement with visible lighting/cage.
  3. Approach: Maintain visual contact with occupants and watch hands.
  4. Contact: Professional greeting, request license/registration, state the reason for the stop.
Use of Force
  • Force must always be proportional to the threat and behavior encountered.
  • Verbal commands come first; escalation must be strictly justified.
05

Module 5: Report Writing & Documentation

The Golden Rule: Accuracy, clarity, and timelines matter more than length. Your report must justify your legal actions.
Core Questions to Answer in Every Report:
1. Why were you there? (Reason for contact/dispatch)
2. What did you observe? (Facts and evidence)
3. What did you do? (Actions taken)
4. Why did you do it? (Legal justification / Probable Cause)
06

Module 6: Scenario Training & After-Action Review (AAR)

During Scenarios

Work through problems methodically. Control the scene before moving to enforcement.

After-Action Review Questions
  1. What was the initial plan?
  2. What changed during the call?
  3. What went well, and what would you do differently next time?

1. Patrol Operations

  • Sign on duty via radio as 10-8 with assigned designator before leaving station.
  • Traffic stops (10-38): Provide location and plate to dispatch prior to approach.

2. Pursuit Directives (10-80)

  • Broadcast direction, speed, and reason for pursuit immediately.
  • Supervisor must assume tactical control of the pursuit channel.

  • Force used must be objectively reasonable based on circumstances.
  • Any discharge requires detailed Internal Use of Force report within 24 hours.

  • SEB captain or senior tactical lead assumes inner perimeter command.
